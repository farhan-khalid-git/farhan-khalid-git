## 1. Agronomy and crop science

- **Field-crop production:** Agronomic management of wheat, rice and maize, including crop establishment, sowing strategies, spacing and stand management.
- **Crop physiology and growth analysis:** Growth indices, yield development, phenology and relationships between crop-management practices and physiological performance.
- **Abiotic stress:** Agronomic approaches to drought, heat and salinity constraints in semi-arid cropping systems.
- **Crop nutrition:** Mineral fertiliser management, nutrient-use efficiency, foliar interventions and soil–plant nutrient relationships.
- **Microbial and organic inputs:** Biofertilisers, plant-growth-promoting microorganisms, composts and other amendments in crop production.
- **Cropping systems:** Intercropping, crop density, resource efficiency and conservation-oriented approaches.

## 2. Experimental design and scientific inference

I emphasise matching each statistical model to the study's **experimental unit, randomisation, replication structure and measured response**.

| Capability area | Methods and considerations |
| --- | --- |
| Experimental design | CRD, RCBD, factorial and split-plot designs; analysis of multi-factor studies |
| Inferential statistics | ANOVA and mixed-effects modelling appropriate to design and treatment structure |
| Assumption checks | Residual behaviour, distributions, variance heterogeneity and model fit |
| Mean comparisons | Estimated marginal means and properly justified comparisons, including protected LSD when applicable |
| Multi-environment studies | Year/environment effects and relevant interactions, with explicit modelling assumptions |
| Data integrity | Treatment coding, outliers, impossible values, missingness, unit consistency and reproducibility checks |
| Multivariate analysis | PCA, correlation analysis, clustering and heatmaps when supported by the scientific question and data |

**Statistical principle:** Software output must be evaluated against the design, assumptions and biological plausibility; attractive figures do not replace correct inference.

## 4. Crop growth analysis and modelling

Areas of interest include:

1. Crop growth rate, relative growth rate, net assimilation rate and leaf-area-based indices.
2. Growth functions, parameter estimation and diagnostics.
3. Time-series or repeated-growth measurements, including experimental-design considerations.
4. Model suitability, uncertainty assessment and numerical checks against reference results.
5. Linking growth analysis to agronomic interpretation and publication-ready presentation.

A current research-software direction is **cropGrowthR**, described separately in [RESEARCH_SOFTWARE.md](RESEARCH_SOFTWARE.md). Planned capabilities are **not** equivalent to completed and independently validated features.

## 5. Research themes and example applications

These are **research areas and study themes**, not a directory of public datasets or downloadable project repositories.

- Rice establishment and seed priming, including comparison of direct seeding and transplanting with different management approaches.
- Wheat performance under delayed sowing and terminal heat, including genotype and management responses.
- Maize production under alternative planting methods and biological inoculation strategies.
- Crop density, nutrient management and maize–soybean intercropping.
- Abiotic-stress management with biological and mineral interventions, supported by physiological and biochemical measurements where relevant.
